# The PARA Method: The Simple System for Organizing Your Digital Life in Seconds (Forte Labs)

Definitive explanation of the [[para-method]] by [[tiago-forte]] (Forte Labs blog, February 24, 2023).

## Core structure

Four categories covering all digital information:
- **Projects** — short-term efforts with goal and end point.
- **Areas** — ongoing responsibilities with no end date.
- **Resources** — topics of interest for future reference.
- **Archives** — inactive items from the above three.

## Key diagnostic

Most project lists contain *areas* masquerading as projects. "Hiring" is an area; "fill three engineering positions by Q3" is a project. Without discrete projects: (1) you can't know the true extent of your commitments, and (2) you can't connect current effort to long-term goals. The endless horizon of areas kills motivation.

## Core principle

Organize by *actionability* — by the project or goal the information serves, not by subject category. "Information should be organized to take you where you want to go."

## On organizational systems

"So much of what we call 'organizing' is essentially procrastination in disguise." PARA is designed to be so simple there is "no excuse and nothing left to do except the next essential step."

## Relation to LLM Wiki

PARA provides the folder/category structure; the [[llm-wiki-pattern]] provides automated maintenance. PARA's "Resources" category most directly maps to the `wiki/` layer — a curated reference collection, maintained by the LLM rather than the user.
