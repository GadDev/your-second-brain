# Second Brain

A personal external knowledge system — a curated digital store of notes, references, and synthesized ideas — that augments human memory and thinking.

## Origin

Coined and popularized by [[tiago-forte]] through his course "Building a Second Brain" (BASB) and his 2022 book of the same name. Forte draws from the idea that our biological brains excel at pattern recognition and creativity but are poor at remembering large numbers of specific details — tasks better delegated to software.

> "Software is always on, never forgets, and can track any number of details on any number of subjects forever." — [[tiago-forte]]

## Traditional approach vs. LLM Wiki

Traditional second-brain systems (Zettelkasten, PARA, BASB) require the *user* to do the maintenance: tagging, linking, summarizing, keeping cross-references current. This is why most personal wikis are abandoned — the maintenance burden exceeds the value.

The [[llm-wiki-pattern]] extends the second-brain concept by delegating all maintenance to an LLM. The human curates sources and asks questions; the LLM does the filing, cross-referencing, and bookkeeping.

## Common failure mode

Most attempts at personal knowledge management fail because organizing is mistaken for thinking. People build elaborate tagging and filing systems, spend hours organizing, and rarely look at the result. As [[tahir-balarabe]] puts it: "organizing isn't thinking. Organizing is just rearranging."

## Scale examples

- [[tiago-forte]]'s BASB course: ~5,000 students
- [[obsidian]]: 1.5 million users (2026)
- [[andrej-karpathy]]'s suggestion: ~100 sources / ~400,000 words is enough to generate complex, synthesized answers
