# Zettelkasten

A personal knowledge management method developed by sociologist Niklas Luhmann (1927-1998), based on atomic, numbered note cards densely linked to each other.

## Core principles

- **Atomicity** — one idea per note.
- **Dense linking** — every note links to related notes; the links are as important as the content.
- **No hierarchical folders** — notes are organized by their connections, not by subject categories.
- **Emergence** — the system surfaces unexpected connections the author didn't plan.

## Modern digital equivalent

Tools like [[obsidian]] implement Zettelkasten digitally via `[[wikilinks]]`. Sönke Ahrens's *How to Take Smart Notes* (referenced by [[tiago-forte]]) popularized Zettelkasten for a modern audience.

## Relation to LLM Wiki

The [[llm-wiki-pattern]] inherits Zettelkasten's emphasis on linking (every concept gets its own page, every mention becomes a `[[wikilink]]`). The key departure: in Zettelkasten, the *human* makes all the links and writes all the notes. In the LLM Wiki pattern, the LLM does the filing, linking, and bookkeeping — the human supplies the sources and the judgment.

The [[ai-first-vault-principle]] by [[eugeniu-ghelbur]] explicitly departs from Zettelkasten: Zettelkasten notes are written for a human to re-read. AI-first notes are written for an LLM to retrieve.
