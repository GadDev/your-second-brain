# PKM

Personal Knowledge Management — the field concerned with how individuals capture, organize, connect, and use the information they encounter over time.

## Key distinction from productivity

PKM is not productivity. Productivity focuses on actions and tasks. PKM focuses on managing the *content* — the stories, evidence, examples, research, and notes that enable creative work. As [[tiago-forte]] puts it: "not everything is a to-do."

## Main traditions

- **Zettelkasten** — Niklas Luhmann's card-based system of atomic, interlinked notes. Influenced most modern digital PKM tools.
- **Building a Second Brain (BASB)** — [[tiago-forte]]'s formalized course and method, with the [[para-method]] as its organizational backbone.
- **Evergreen notes** — Andy Matuschak's approach: notes as living, evolving artifacts linked densely to each other.
- **Digital gardens** — Anne-Laure Le Cunff's framing of personal wikis as "private gardens where plants grow and entangle in unexpected ways."

## Biggest misconception

PKM is not a technical, formal practice. It is inherently messy and informal. There is no "correct" way; the goal is to be more spontaneous and creative, not locked down by rigid rules.

## LLM era shift

The [[llm-wiki-pattern]] represents a shift in PKM: instead of the human doing the maintenance (the part everyone abandons), the LLM does it. Human role: curate sources, ask questions, think. LLM role: summarize, cross-reference, file, keep consistent.

Tools that rely on plain text formats (especially [[obsidian]]) benefited most from this shift — no migration needed when AI coding agents arrived.
